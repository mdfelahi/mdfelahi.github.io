Summary

I am a PhD candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Purdue University specializing in Human-Centered AI, Human-Automation Interaction, and Cognitive Engineering. My research focuses on designing socially intelligent, explainable computer systems—such as automated vehicles and user interfaces—that understand nuances in human behavior and proactively interact with humans. Working with my advisor, Dr. Renran Tian, at the Intelligent Cognitive and Ergonomics Laboratory (ICEL) at North Carolina State University, I conduct interdisciplinary research grounded in behavioral science, cognitive psychology, and machine learning. Additionally, I have been serving as the lead instructor for undergraduate laboratory courses at Purdue ECE for the past four semesters, demonstrating a strong foundation in teaching and technical leadership.

Research Experience

Adaptive In-Vehicle UI Feedback via Decision Entropy

  • Challenge: UIs with the same depth of information miscalibrates user information needs—overloading in simple scenarios, under-informing in complex pedestrian encounters.
  • Modeling Scene Complexity: Defined Perceived Scene Complexity (PSC) as Driving Decision Entropy (DDE)—the statistical diversity in driver action choices.
  • Intent-Uncertainty Link: Higher pedestrian intent uncertainty strongly correlates with higher DDE, showing behavioral ambiguity increases decision diversity.
  • Dynamic Feedback Scaling: Evaluated three Situational Awareness (SA)-level interface concepts: Perception, Comprehension, and Projection.
  • Key Findings: Perception-only feedback suits low-entropy scenarios; richer SA-specific details preferred as complexity rises. Predictive feedback reduces cognitive load and improves perceived system performance.
  • Contribution: Empirical foundation for Adaptive UI where feedback detail scales with real-time decision diversity to enhance transparency and reduce cognitive workload.

Driver Temporal Segmentation of Pedestrian Intentions

  • The Challenge: Static AI intent labels fail to capture the continuous, dynamic updates human drivers make during real-time road negotiations.
  • Common Boundaries: Using DBSCAN clustering, this research identifies temporal "breakpoints" where 72% of drivers simultaneously update their assessment of a pedestrian's intent, typically within a 0.5-second window.
  • Clarification vs. Confusion: 75% of these shared boundaries act as "clarifying events" (e.g., lane proximity), while ambiguous cues (e.g., slow movement) trigger "confusion boundaries" where driver interpretations diverge.
  • Trait-Driven Dynamics: While personality does not affect initial accuracy, traits like impulsiveness and aggressiveness significantly dictate how frequently and quickly a driver re-segments their understanding of the scene.
  • Impact: Provides a framework for automated vehicles to recognize human cognitive breakpoints, enabling more natural and predictable AI-pedestrian negotiation.

Quantifying AI Social Aptitude in Automated Driving

  • The Challenge: Traditional performance metrics like accuracy fail to capture the "social intelligence" required for AI to interact naturally and predictably with pedestrians.
  • The "Human-Likeness" Gap: AI models often reach the correct final prediction while following temporal reasoning patterns that humans find counterintuitive or unstable.
  • The Human-Like Index (HL-i): This research introduces the HL-i metric, which uses time-series clustering to quantify the alignment between AI and human prediction trajectories.
  • Experimental Findings: AI predictions deviated significantly from human decision-making patterns in over 50% of tested scenarios. Transformer-based architectures (TrEP) demonstrated a higher degree of human-likeness compared to traditional LSTM models (PIE).
  • Validation: A Turing-inspired study confirmed that humans find it significantly more difficult to distinguish AI predictions from human ones when the HL-i score is high.
  • Impact: The framework provides a path toward developing "socially apt" AI that is more trustworthy and aligned with human social cognition.

MindReaD Annotation Tool: Capturing Micro-Level Reasoning Data

  • The Challenge: Safety-critical ML models require "commonsense reasoning" data that connects human cognitive states to specific visual objects, which traditional tools fail to capture.
  • The MindReaD Framework: A novel annotation tool that captures human knowledge at three granularities: Event level (timestamps), Context level (textual rationale), and Entity level (grounding text to visual objects).
  • Point-and-Explain (PaE): Implements an interaction design that mimics natural human explanation strategies, allowing annotators to link reasoning text directly to visual representations.
  • Key Findings: The PaE feature and multi-prompt UI encouraged annotators to include significantly more reasoning factors compared to baseline designs. Training an AI model with micro-level reasoning annotations resulted in a 10% improvement in intent prediction accuracy over traditional models.
  • Contribution: Establishes a methodology for acquiring high-fidelity rationale data, bridging the gap between raw video data and human-like algorithmic reasoning.

Investigating Crowd's Ability for Predicting Pedestrian's Crossing Intent

  • Designed large-scale crowdsourcing experiments (N = 120) to evaluate prediction accuracy at 1s, 3s, and 5s horizons.
  • Investigated cognitive bias (status quo bias) in driver intent prediction.
  • Compared human prediction stability against algorithmic models over longer horizons.
  • Demonstrated improved accuracy through task framing adjustments (53% to 79% at 1s horizon).

Transforming Natural Text Description to Structured Data

  • The Challenge: Converting unstructured human reasoning (natural language) into structured data for training AI models.
  • The IE Framework: Adapts a biLSTM-CRF algorithm to automatically map free-form text into structured data objects.
  • Knowledge Categorization: Extracts 24 critical factors across six categories (Dynamic, Traffic, Pedestrian States, Social, etc.).
  • Impact: Bridges qualitative human observations and quantitative AI training, allowing models to learn "the why" behind human decisions.
