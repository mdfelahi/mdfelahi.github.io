Summary

I am an electrical and computer engineering educator and a human-centered AI researcher. At Elizabethtown College, I teach Circuit Analysis with its laboratory as a flipped, mastery-based course, and in Spring 2027 I will teach Electronics with its laboratory and Electromagnetism and co-supervise sophomore community-based design projects. Teaching Approach

My goal is to help every student build a growth mindset: the belief that ability grows with effort, feedback, and persistence. I start with theory so students can reason through a design before they build it, and five strategies guide my teaching.

1Structured Lectures and Active Learning

  • Theory first, introduced through everyday devices
  • Derivations on the board, with notes taken by hand
  • Short in-class quizzes (Top Hat, Canvas)

2Flexible Assessment That Rewards Mastery

  • Mastery-based grading with retakes
  • Grades spread across quizzes, labs, projects, and exams
  • Optional bonus assignments as a second chance

3Labs and Group Projects That Test Theory

  • Predict, simulate, build, measure, and explain
  • Theory-guided troubleshooting
  • Group projects with oral presentations

4Student Support and Inclusive Course Design

  • About six help hours a week, plus email and online meetings
  • Progress logs to reach struggling students early
  • Universal Design for Learning and transparent assignments (TILT)

5Continuous Improvement Through Feedback

  • Mid-term anonymous GIFT survey: what helps, what hinders, what to change
  • Results shared with the class and acted on
  • Peer feedback through a faculty Teaching Circle

Research

My research builds human-centered AI that understands how people think and explains its decisions clearly. My dissertation studied how autonomous vehicles can understand human cognition and explain their decisions to passengers. Across these projects, I ran human-subject studies with over 350 participants in the lab and through crowdsourcing. Going forward, I plan to involve undergraduates in building affordable, human-centered technology: private AI assistants that run on low-cost personal devices, student-built devices that serve real users, and engineering education studies.
